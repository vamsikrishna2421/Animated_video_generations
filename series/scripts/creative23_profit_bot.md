# Creative 23 "Three Lakhs on the Screen" (the Nov 8 creative slot) · script v2, Nov 8

## The idea (point family G, scams dressed as AI; last G post: EP45, Nov 3)
"AI trading bot" is a costume. No AI, and no real investment, pays a fixed profit every day. The app's balance that
rises exactly 3% a day is a formula, not trading: ₹2,00,000 × 1.03, fifteen times = ₹3,11,593. If it were real, ₹2 lakh
would be about ₹970 crore in a year. The real product is the "tax" or "fee" it asks before you can withdraw. The tells:
a fixed daily profit, a fee to withdraw. What to do: don't pay; check the name on SEBI's site; call 1930 /
cybercrime.gov.in.

Line 1 says both numbers within 2 s (EN and TE).

## Facts and sources (checked Nov 8, 2026)
- SEBI: "no guarantees of assured returns in the securities market" (investor caution, Feb 2024: Business Standard,
  Moneylife); its June 2022 caution (PR 20/2022) on unregulated platforms selling algo-trading strategies with claims of
  huge returns (Business Standard, Taxmann, Free Press Journal). Registration can be checked on SEBI's list of registered
  intermediaries (sebi.gov.in).
- The pattern in 2026 police cases: WhatsApp/Telegram groups, a fake trading app with a growing dashboard, a small early
  withdrawal for trust, then a "tax"/"fee" before any big withdrawal (Free Press Journal: Mumbai, Malad, Mar 2026: asked
  ₹5 lakh to "activate" an ₹18 lakh withdrawal; Mumbai, Prabhadevi: a 5% "tax"; Mumbai: the victim's chartered accountant
  told him no such tax is needed to withdraw; Indore: a "cross-country tax"; Etemaad Daily: Hyderabad, an "AI trading"
  scam). Press reports of police complaints.
- 1930: the national cyber-fraud helpline run by I4C (Ministry of Home Affairs) since April 2022, and
  cybercrime.gov.in (Kerala Police, India TV, Vikaspedia).
- Our numbers (arithmetic): 2,00,000 × 1.03^15 = 3,11,593; 18% of it = 56,087; 2,00,000 × 1.03^365 = 969.7 crore.
- Sastry garu, Pallavi and "AI Profit Bot" are made up (no real app or brand).

## Script review (human-viewer, v1) and what changed
Both stop on frame 1 (hook 8 and 8), the verdict at 4 s doesn't lose them because the dad argues back, "970 crore" lands,
and both would forward it to family (Sandeep the Telugu one, Priya the English one to her parents). Both drop at the
spoken "times one point zero three, fifteen times" maths (line 5); nobody says whose ₹2 lakh or why fifteen days; Pallavi's
CA job is never shown; the rule card gets cut by the joke. Sandeep: frame 1 never says "AI" (a bank or UPI problem?); 3% sounds
small next to nothing; "tax" vs "fee" mixed; check what on SEBI's site?; is his ₹2 lakh gone? Priya: "no real investment
pays a fixed profit every day" is false (FDs and savings accounts do; SEBI's quote is about the securities market); the
formula match isn't proof (an FD matches its formula too): fifteen days with no down day is; 18% "tax" on his own ₹2 lakh;
the rule order is wrong for a dad who has already paid: call 1930 first, check SEBI before investing; fake apps borrow real
firms' names. Telugu: line 2 bookish ("FD ఇస్తుంది కదా"), line 5 cut short, line 7 too literal, line 8 says 900 while the
screen says 970, "ఆ ఫీజే" → "ఆ టాక్సే", "విత్ డ్రా చేసే ముందు", "వన్ నైన్ త్రీ జీరో", "ఏఐ" may come out "ఏయ్".
Changed (v2): line 2 "No real trading makes a profit every single day"; line 3 says whose money and when ("Two lakhs,
fifteen days ago. Three percent profit, every single day!"); line 4 makes the no-down-day curve the proof ("Fifteen days,
and not one bad day? Real markets fall some days."); line 5 says no long number (the screen shows it); "To the rupee?!";
the green block "THE AI APP SAYS HE HAS"; the speaker tag "Pallavi, CA"; "tax" everywhere; line 10 "Real tax goes to the
government, not to the app. No real broker holds your money until you pay."; the rule: "Don't pay more. Call one nine
three zero now. And before you invest, check SEBI's site." and the card holds ~3 s before the ending; a drop before
"970 crore". Telugu as listed. Not changed: the FD comparison stays out of the lines (rates move; "a few percent" adds a
claim); the 18% on his own money is on the pop-up for viewers to spot.
