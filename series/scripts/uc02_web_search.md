# UC02 "₹168, With a Link": how an AI answers with web search (queue 065)

A teaching skit in Ravi anna's shop (ROUTINE.md Run 1). Cast: Ravi anna and the shop's phone AI, now with web search
(made up; our test is real). Topic (SYLLABUS UC02): search tool → read pages → cite the answer. Point family M (give it
the source, check the source), last used by EP53 (Nov 15); the posts before it are Creative 38 (S) and UC01 (H).
Bujji's reads applied: frame 1 money-first, a same-item clash; steps-first middle (as UC01); the quiz early enough to
frame the answer; no comment ask (the try-it in the caption).

## Facts
- Our test (`series/scripts/uc02_demo/README.md`; both scripts committed before they ran): a made-up mini web of six
  dated pages, a keyword search tool, a small free AI on our computer, 10 fresh chats each. The top result is an old
  page whose title says "today": ₹168. Today's page (dated 24 Nov 2026): ₹142. With the old page undated: told today's
  date, 10/10 answered ₹142 from today's page; not told the date, 10/10 answered ₹168 and cited the old page, which
  really does say ₹168. With no search: 10/10 "I don't have real-time access".
- Columbia Journalism Review / Tow Center, March 2025: eight AI search tools, asked to name the source of news quotes,
  were wrong in more than 60% of 1,600 queries (also Nieman Lab, Fortune).

## Hook options
1. **Chosen: the same-item money clash.** Frame 1: Ravi with a dal sack and a bill; blocks "SHOP AI (WITH A LINK) ₹168/kg"
   (red) vs "BEGUM BAZAR TODAY ₹142/kg" (green); a chip "50 kg · ₹1,300 extra". Line 1: "One sixty-eight a kilo, with a
   link! The market today was one forty-two!"
2. Count clash: "NOT TOLD THE DATE 10/10 WRONG" vs "TOLD THE DATE 10/10 RIGHT": strong but no person at stake.
3. The link itself in frame 1 ("hyderabad-prices.../toor-dal-...-today"): too much text.

## Script review (human-viewer, v1) and what changed
- Both: "nobody told me today's date" left "doesn't my phone know the date?" open → Ravi asks it, and the AI answers
  what we tested: the phone knows; the AI knows only what the app tells it (no claim about the big apps' prompts). The
  quiz answer was obvious (it had "date" in it) → A trust the link, B ask again, C open the link; B is the trap, and
  the answer says why: asked again, it said ₹168 every time (10 fresh chats). The rule covers the undated page and
  "today" → "Open the link. 'Today' is not a date. No date, no trust." "Where, not when" (Priya's pick) stays as the
  answer's line and a chip. The study shrinks to one line and one big number, with the spread (best 37%, worst 94%).
- Priya: "we tested it" from the AI broke the skit → the AI speaks in the first person (it is the small AI on the
  shop's computer, as in UC01); the test card says it plainly and runs longer, bigger; the builder note ("put today's
  date in its instructions") goes on screen; no bonk on 60% (a buzz); the study is a different failure (a wrong source,
  not an old page) → it is framed as "and the big apps?", "got the source wrong".
- Sandeep: the real twist gets no hit → the bed drops before "But this app never told me today's date"; fewer pops on
  the step boxes. Telugu: "లింక్ కూడా ఇచ్చింది!", "తేదీ చెప్పకుండా పది సార్లు / తేదీ చెప్పి పది సార్లు",
  "అరవై శాతానికి పైగా", "ట్వంటీ ట్వంటీ ఫైవ్ లో"; options said as ఒకటి / రెండు / మూడు and the answer "మూడోది!" (a lone
  letter is off the Telugu rules; numbers on the Telugu screen).
