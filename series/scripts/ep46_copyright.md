# EP46 "Copyright & AI" (Season 5: Responsible AI) · queue 049 · script v1, Nov 4

## The lesson
Who owns what an AI makes? In the US, nobody owns the AI's part: copyright needs a human author, and prompts alone
aren't enough (an award-winning AI picture made with 624 prompts was refused). In India it isn't settled. And the AI
learned from artists' work, which India's government is proposing to pay them for. What you can do: say you used AI,
put your own hand in it (that part is yours), keep your drafts.

## The reach stack (Standing Rule; life-first, as EP44 and EP45)

2. Two clashing numbers in frame 1, readable with the sound off: 40 HOURS (drawn by hand, 2nd, ₹0) vs 40 SECONDS (AI, 1st,
   ₹10,000).
3. Injustice: the girl who drew it loses to the boy who typed it. The AI is the culprit, the contest rules the gap.
Setting: a college (the routine: work and study topics move to an office or a college).

## Facts and sources (checked Nov 4, 2026)
- US Copyright Office, "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability" (Jan 29, 2025): "given current
  generally available technology, prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make users of an AI system
  the authors of the output"; human-authored parts (own drawing, selection and arrangement, edits) can be protected.
- US Court of Appeals, D.C. Circuit, Thaler v. Perlmutter (Mar 18, 2025): the Copyright Act requires human authorship.
- "Théâtre D'opéra Spatial" (Jason Allen): won at the 2022 Colorado State Fair; made with at least 624 Midjourney
  prompts; the Copyright Office Review Board refused registration on Sep 5, 2023 because he would not disclaim the
  AI-made parts; he has sued (Colorado federal court). We don't show the picture.
- India, Copyright Act 1957, Section 2(d)(vi): for a computer-generated work, the author is "the person who causes the
  work to be created"; not yet tested on today's generative AI. DPIIT committee, Working Paper Part 1 (Dec 8, 2025):
  proposes a mandatory blanket licence for AI training with royalties to copyright holders (a proposal, not law); Part 2,
  on who owns AI outputs, was still to come at our check.
