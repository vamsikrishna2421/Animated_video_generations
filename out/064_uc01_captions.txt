A ₹1,928 phone order. The shop's AI took 42 seconds to say a word. She hung up.

Use case 1 of AI From Scratch: how an AI phone-call assistant works
- Every turn has three steps, one after the other: ears (her voice → text), brain (an AI writes the reply), mouth (the reply → speech).
- Our test, on a small computer with no GPU (one ₹1,928 order, 6 calls each): ears 1.8 s, brain 29.6 s (it writes the whole reply first), mouth 10.1 s. 41.5 s of silence. People leave about 0.2 s between turns.
- "Just answer shorter": 7 seconds, and the total was wrong in 6 of 6 (₹2,156 to ₹2,450). Without writing the sum out, it didn't do the sum.
- A smaller model: faster, right in 1 of 6. Three times it charged only for the rice.
- Talk while thinking: say the first sentence while the rest is written. First sound at about 2.5 s (worked out from our timings), total right 6 of 6. Still slower than a person, but she hears you.
- Big apps on fast chips: OpenAI says its old three-step voice mode took 2.8-5.4 s on average; its GPT-4o answers in about 0.32 s (its own figures, 2024).

Try it: call any AI phone line or voice assistant and count the seconds before it speaks. Then ask it to add three prices, and check the total.

What to do if you build one:
1. Stream it: speak the first sentence while the rest is still being written
2. Never cut the working to save time; for money, check the total with a calculator or a tool
3. Next problem: interruptions. A good phone AI stops talking when the caller talks.
