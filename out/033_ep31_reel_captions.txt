=== CAPTION ===
We asked a tiny free AI to turn WhatsApp orders into neat order slips: name, phone, items, address. It just copied them back. 0 out of 30. A better prompt with two examples got 16. Then we taught it with LoRA on 240 made-up orders: 14 minutes on 4 CPU cores, no GPU. 27 out of 30 perfect, on orders it never saw.

Episode 31 of AI From Scratch: teach an AI a new skill without a supercomputer (LoRA)
- Why a tiny model and not ChatGPT? It's free, runs on your own computer, and your customers' details never leave it
- Normal training changes every number inside the model (360 million in ours), and every new skill means a whole new copy
- LoRA's trick: freeze the original, stick a few new numbers on top (like sticky notes in a textbook), train only those
- In our test the notes were 1.2% of the numbers, saved as a 9 MB skill file next to a 724 MB model
- Apple says its iPhone AI works the same way: one model, a small skill file for each feature

Your turn, answered: B, give it the list. Prices that change every week go in with the question, not into LoRA notes: you'd retrain every week, and it can still misremember a price. LoRA is for skills and style.

Before you train anything: 1) prompt first (two examples took ours from 0 to 16), 2) facts that keep changing? put them in the prompt, 3) a skill the prompt can't nail? a small LoRA. Then check its work: 3 of our 30 slips were still wrong (one phone number, two item lists).

Bigger models work too: QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023) fine-tuned a 65-billion-parameter model on a single 48 GB GPU.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
How we ran it: SmolLM2-360M-Instruct (Hugging Face, Apache-2.0). Prompt: "Turn this WhatsApp order into an order slip: JSON with name, phone, items (item, qty) and address." The orders were made up by us, so real chats will be messier. 240 training orders, 2 passes, LoRA rank 8 on every layer (1.2% of the numbers), 14 minutes on 4 CPU cores, no GPU. Tested on 30 new orders: plain prompt 0/30, two worked examples in the prompt 16/30, LoRA 27/30, about 7 seconds per order on the same CPU. We didn't test a big chatbot on these 30; the point of a tiny model is free, private and yours. The order in the video is one more run with a made-up phone number (55555 01234). LoRA needs a model whose weights you can train: an open model, or a service that offers fine-tuning. What skill would you teach a small AI?

=== SOURCES (for comments) ===
Demo (run by us): HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct, Apache-2.0. Synthetic WhatsApp-style orders (Indian names, Hyderabad/AP addresses, mixed quantity spellings); 240 to train, 30 new ones to test; "perfect" = name, phone, every item with its quantity and the address all exactly right. LoRA rank 8 on all linear layers, 2 passes, 14 minutes on 4 CPU cores. Plain prompt 0/30 (it mostly copied the order back), two worked examples in the prompt 16/30, LoRA 27/30 (the same with the skill file saved in 16-bit, 8.7 MB; the model file is 723.7 MB).
LoRA: Hu et al. (Microsoft), "LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models", arXiv 2106.09685 (ICLR 2022): the pretrained weights stay frozen; small trainable low-rank matrices are added.
Apple: "Introducing Apple's On-Device and Server Foundation Models" (Apple Machine Learning Research, 10 Jun 2024): "Apple Intelligence includes a broad set of adapters, each fine-tuned for a specific feature."
QLoRA: Dettmers et al., arXiv 2305.14314 (2023): a 65B model fine-tuned on a single 48 GB GPU (regular 16-bit fine-tuning needs more than 780 GB of GPU memory).
Prompt first, then context, then fine-tuning: OpenAI, "Optimizing LLM accuracy"; Google Cloud model tuning docs ("start with prompting").
Model: SmolLM2 (Allal et al., 2025), Hugging Face.
