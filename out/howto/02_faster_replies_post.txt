=== CAPTION ===
In our test, a small free AI took 71 seconds to write a two-line WhatsApp message. Three settings got the same message to 5 seconds.

1. Less thinking (the model menu)
ChatGPT: Instant (on the free plan Instant is the default: just don't tap Think)
Gemini: a Flash model (if you see a Thinking level, pick the lowest; on the free plan there may be no menu at all)
Claude: click the model name → Effort → Low (thinking can't be switched off on Claude's current models, only turned down)
2. Ask for a short reply: add "Keep it to 2 short lines." to your question.
3. New topic, new chat. Type the one detail it needs (your shop's name) instead of keeping one month-long chat.

Our test: a small free AI (Qwen3-4B, offline, on a 4-core computer). The apps run on big servers and are much faster; what carries over is which settings cost time. The same WhatsApp job, the whole reply, 3 runs each (the middle one):
thinking on: 71 s → thinking off: 15 s → + a short reply: 5 s (our test switched thinking off; Claude's Low effort still thinks a little, so expect less of a cut there)
The same job at the end of a month-long chat (90 messages), thinking on: 132 s. In the apps a long chat slows down less (they keep part of it ready), but it uses more of your limit (Claude's help page).
Keep thinking for hard jobs: maths, planning, code. For messages and replies, fast is enough.

Apps rename these menus often: if yours looks different, pick the fast or lowest option.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
The exact taps (checked Oct 29, 2026):
ChatGPT: the model menu → Instant. Free and Go plans: Instant is already on; Think turns deeper reasoning on for one message, so leave it off for quick jobs.
Gemini: the model menu → Flash. If a "Thinking level" shows at the bottom of the menu, choose the lowest.
Claude (claude.ai): click the model name next to the send button → Effort → Low. "Higher effort means more thorough responses, but they take longer and use more tokens" (Claude's help page).
How we tested: Qwen3-4B (Qwen's own 4-bit file, Apache 2.0), llama.cpp, 4 CPU cores, the internet cut, Qwen's recommended settings, 3 runs per setting from an empty cache. The job: "Write a WhatsApp message for my shop's customers: the shop is closed this Sunday for a family function, and open again on Monday at 7 am." Every reply read by hand: all 15 say the shop is closed on Sunday and opens on Monday at 7 am (6 say "this Sunday", the rest "Sunday" or "on Sunday"). Noticed while reading: the short replies were ready to paste; the long ones had "[Your Shop Name]" blanks, "Subject:" lines and notes about the message. With thinking on, asking for a short reply alone was erratic (29, 36 and 90 s): turn thinking down first.
