Episode 36 of AI From Scratch: GPUs and AI chips, why hardware decides AI
- Every word an AI writes is over a billion small multiplications (EP35's medium brain has 1.5 billion parameters, and a model does about one multiply-add per parameter for every word piece)
- A CPU has a few fast cores (a phone or laptop: 4 to 16). A graphics card (GPU) has thousands of small ones working at once: a gaming laptop's RTX 5060 has 3,328 cores and 8 GB of memory (NVIDIA's spec page)
- Our real test, the same small AI writing the same reply on our laptop's CPU: 1 core 11.5 s, 2 cores 6.3 s, 4 cores 3.6 s. More cores make even one reply faster, because each word's work is split between them. But 23 orders still take about 83 s
- The brain must fit in the card's memory: the big brain at full size is about 15 GB, and an 8 GB card can't hold it, so the rest runs on the CPU again: slow

What this means for you:
1. ChatGPT, Gemini and the rest run on the companies' own graphics cards and AI chips. You don't need one to use them
2. To run AI on your own machine, look at the graphics card and its memory (GB), not the CPU's GHz. The model has to fit in that memory
3. No graphics card? Google Colab is free to use, though its free GPUs are "heavily restricted" and not guaranteed (Google's own FAQ). Your phone's "AI chip" (NPU) is a small version of the same idea: many small workers at once

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
How we tested (run by us): EP35's medium brain (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Apache 2.0, Qwen's own 4-bit GGUF file) with llama.cpp on our laptop's CPU, no graphics card. The same order ("Anna, please send 2 kg rice, 1 litre sunflower oil, 6 eggs and 1 packet of tea powder to flat 302"), the same 55-word-piece reply every time (greedy decoding), the context cleared before each run, median of 3 runs: 1 core 11.5 s, 2 cores 6.3 s, 4 cores 3.6 s for the whole reply (3.15x faster on 4 cores, not quite 4x: the cores also wait for the numbers to arrive from memory).
We have no graphics card here, so the scene where Sunny's gaming laptop clears the queue is an illustration, not a measurement.
GPU numbers: NVIDIA's own spec pages: GeForce RTX 5060 Laptop GPU, 3,328 CUDA cores, 8 GB GDDR7; GeForce RTX 5090 (desktop), 21,760 CUDA cores, 32 GB. The big brain at full size: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct has 7.61 billion parameters (model card), about 15 GB at 16 bits each; Qwen's 4-bit file of it is 4.68 GB (EP35). "About one multiply-add per parameter per token": Kaplan et al., "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models" (OpenAI, 2020).
