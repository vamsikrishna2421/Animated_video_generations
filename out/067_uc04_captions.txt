Use case 4 of AI From Scratch: how YouTube and Netflix recommend videos
- To the app, every video is a list of numbers: a point on a map. Videos that are alike sit close together.
- Up next shows the videos closest to the one playing now (YouTube's own page: Up next "suggests additional content based on what you're currently watching").
- Your home page is built from your whole history: you are, roughly, the average of everything you watch (the idea in Google's 2016 YouTube paper).

Our test (a small recommender we built on our computer, 144 made-up videos, Ravi's made-up history of 12):
- Up next while the friend's trading video plays: 9 of 10 trading.
- Autoplay, 10 in a row: 8 of 10 trading.
- His home page after those 10: 2 of 10 trading with recent videos counted more (0 of 10 with every video counted the same). Netflix's help page says recent viewing outweighs older viewing.
- Up next while a scam-warning video plays: 9 of 10 trading tips. Ours matches titles; YouTube learns closeness from what people watch, so the real app may differ.

Try it: open one video you'd never watch and count how many of the next ten are like it. Then switch autoplay off (the switch on the video player) and tap ⋮ → Not interested on the ones you don't want, right in Up next.
