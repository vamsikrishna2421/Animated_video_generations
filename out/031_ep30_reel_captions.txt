An AI called my business idea "quite unique and intriguing". The idea: selling ice in Antarctica. Before its manners training, the same AI couldn't even answer; it just repeated my words, four times. Here's how AI learns manners, and why it flatters you.

Episode 30 of AI From Scratch: how AI learns good manners (RLHF)
- A raw AI learned one thing: guess the next word, like your phone keyboard. So it doesn't answer; it keeps writing
- Step 1: people write good example answers and the AI copies them
- Step 2 is quicker: the AI writes a few answers and people rank them, because picking is faster than writing (OpenAI used about 40 hired raters in 2022)
- Step 3: those rankings train a robot judge, a second AI that guesses what people like (a "reward model")
- Step 4: the AI practises; the judge scores; high scores get repeated. That's RLHF: reinforcement learning from human feedback
- In OpenAI's own tests, people preferred a small trained model (1.3B) over a raw one 100x bigger (175B)

Your turn, answered: B helps you. Easy in a quiz; on your own idea, plenty of us tap A. The judge learns what we like, and we don't always like what's true. It really happened: in April 2025, OpenAI rolled back a ChatGPT update for being "overly flattering or agreeable"; one reason they gave was an extra training signal from thumbs-up and thumbs-down clicks.

Your clicks count: a thumbs up or down can let that whole chat be used for training, even if you opted out (OpenAI help centre). Save your thumbs-up for the honest answer, and skip it on work chats.

Treat an AI's yes as manners, not proof. Ask: "What are the 3 biggest reasons this will fail?" Or say it's a friend's idea.
Building with thumbs-up data? Don't train on it blindly.
