Use case 3 of AI From Scratch: how AI meeting notes work
- Three steps: ears (speech-to-text turn the call into text), who said what (names on the lines), then an AI writes the notes and the action items.
- Each step passes its mistakes on. In our test (a made-up supplier call, a small free AI on our computer, 10 runs each), the ears heard "fourteen fifty rupees a bag" as "14.50 rupees": 7 of 10 notes wrote ₹14.50 a bag, 5 of 10 totalled the order at ₹580 (the real order: ₹58,000).
- Without the dollar or rupee word, the ears wrote "$14.50", and 7 of 10 notes copied the dollars.
- With no names on who said what, 6 of 10 notes gave Ravi's job (keep 3 empty sacks ready) to the supplier. With names, 10 of 10 got it right. Names fix who, not money.
- Said in full ("one thousand four hundred and fifty rupees"), the ears wrote 1,450, and not one note got the price wrong.

Try it: on your next call that an app will summarise, say the amounts in full, and read every number and every name in the notes before you forward them.

The big apps say it too: Google's help page says Meet's "Take notes for me" summary "can be incomplete, inaccurate, or not generated", and lists 8 languages (Telugu and Hindi aren't among them). Zoom's meeting summary lists Telugu and Hindi. We didn't test either app.

Building one? A "prices are in rupees" hint to speech-to-text took the dollar sign away, but 8 of 10 notes still wrote 14.50: check amounts against the call.
