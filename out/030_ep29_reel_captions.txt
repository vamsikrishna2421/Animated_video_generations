Episode 29 of AI From Scratch: how AI sees a photo (computer vision)
- Pixels: a photo is a grid of pixels, each one 3 numbers (red, green, blue, 0 to 255). Our small photo is 150,528 numbers; a 12 MP phone photo is about 36 million. Not one of them says "dog"
- Patterns: tiny pattern-finders (called filters) slide over the numbers, each looking for one thing, like an edge. This AI learned its filters from 1.28 million labelled photos
- Layers: edges build parts (eyes, ears), parts build a face, and out comes an answer: "golden retriever"
- Your turn, answered: nudge just as many numbers, just as tiny, but randomly, and it still says dog (5 tries out of 5)
- Why ours worked: the AI is free to download, so we looked inside it and moved 122,204 of the 150,528 numbers by exactly 1, each one toward "banana". Pushes that all aim the same way add up; random ones cancel out

It only fooled this one AI: four other free AI models still said dog, and saving the photo as a JPEG broke the trick. (We couldn't aim it at ChatGPT: it's closed, so nobody outside can look inside it.) But "99.99% sure" was still wrong, and chatbots don't even show a number. Double-check what matters.

Tried the banana photo in ChatGPT or Google Lens? Tell us what it said.

=== SOURCES (for comments) ===
Photo: "Golden Retriever 88.JPG" by Mtlchung, CC0 (Wikimedia Commons), cropped to the 224 × 224 pixels the AI was given.
Models (free, run by us): microsoft/resnet-50 (Apache-2.0; trained on ImageNet-1k, 1.28 million labelled photos). Transfer check: google/vit-base-patch16-224, microsoft/resnet-18, facebook/convnext-tiny-224, microsoft/swin-tiny-patch4-window7-224 all still say golden retriever on the banana file.
The banana file: a targeted gradient attack (projected gradient descent, 80 steps) with every pixel value allowed to move by at most 1 out of 255; 122,204 of the 150,528 values moved by 1, the rest stayed. ResNet-50 then says banana at over 99.99%. Random ±1 changes to the same share of values (5 tries): golden retriever every time. The banana file saved as JPEG (quality 75 to 95): golden retriever again.
Light-up maps: real first-layer responses of ResNet-50 on the photo, brightened so they are visible.
