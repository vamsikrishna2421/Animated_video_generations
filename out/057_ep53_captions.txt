Episode 53 of AI From Scratch: build RAG over your PDFs, Part 2 (Season 6, a screen tutorial)
1. Turn every page into numbers once (an "embedding": 384 numbers a page with the free model all-MiniLM-L6-v2). 300 pages took 3.1 seconds on our computer.
2. Turn the question into numbers too, and take the 3 closest pages. "Jumping and banging when it spins" found the page that says "loud knocking noises during the spin": different words, same meaning.
3. Send just those pages, each marked with its page number, with Part 1's rules (answer only from the document, say the page, or say "Not in the document").

Our test, a 300-page manual we wrote for a made-up washing machine, ten questions with the right page fixed first (a small test, on one small free model on our computer):
- right page in the top 3, by meaning: 8 of 10
- Part 1's word search, top 3: 7 of 10 (in Part 1 it got 1 of 10 because it took only the top page, the cover)
- both mixed, the simple way (reciprocal rank fusion): 6 of 10. Mixing isn't automatically better: test it.
Of the 8 found, 7 answers cite the right page and 1 cites a copy of it. The 2 misses both said "Not in the document" (the child lock is on page 96). Taking more pages wouldn't have fixed it: page 96 ranked 15th of 300. So when it says "not there", check the PDF yourself.

Which one for your PDF? A few pages, fits with room to spare: send it whole, with page numbers (Part 1). Long, or lots of questions: search by meaning and send the top 3 (Part 2). Either way, test it with 10 questions whose page you already know. No code? Upload the PDF to a chat app and run the same 10 questions.
(The whole manual is 62 times the tokens of the 3 pages, with every question, and you pay for every token: money on a paid AI, waiting time on your own computer. We set our small AI to read 4,096 tokens at once, so the whole manual didn't fit at all. Our made-up pages are short, about 50 tokens each; split long pages into chunks first.)

The code (Python; pip install transformers torch pypdf):
pages = read_pdf("manual.pdf")   # Part 1, step 1 (pypdf, one text per page)
embed = embedder()   # all-MiniLM-L6-v2, mean pooling, unit-length vectors
page_vecs = embed(pages)   # 300 pages, once
q_vec = embed([question])[0]
scores = (page_vecs @ q_vec).tolist()   # cosine similarity
top3 = sorted(range(len(pages)), key=lambda i: -scores[i])[:3]   # best first
doc = "\n\n".join(f"--- page {i+1} ---\n{pages[i]}" for i in top3)
messages = [{"role": "system", "content": RULES}, {"role": "user", "content": f"Document:\n{doc}\n\nQuestion: {question}"}]
How we tested (Nov 15, 2026): a 300-page manual for a made-up "WM-900" washing machine (programs, error codes, parts and prices, warranty, service), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (4-bit) on llama.cpp's OpenAI-compatible server with a 4,096-token context setting, temperature 0.2; embeddings with sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (texts cut at 256 tokens, so split long pages); ten everyday questions with the right page fixed before the run; every reply read by hand. Whole manual: 15,373 tokens. The 3 pages: 197 to 252 prompt tokens a question (the drain question: 247). Word search = the page sharing the most rare words; the mix = reciprocal rank fusion of both full rankings (k = 60), which lost two pages that meaning had found. Where the right page ranked by meaning (rank_check.py): 8 questions 1st or 2nd; the child lock page 15th, the smell page 88th, so top 5 or top 10 would not have caught them. Bigger models and better embedding models may do better; count your own.
