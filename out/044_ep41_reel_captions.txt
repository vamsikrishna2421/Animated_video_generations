Episode 41 of AI From Scratch: how to choose the right AI model
- "Biggest" isn't a job. Write the pass mark first, before you compare anything (OpenAI's own model guide: "Choose acceptance criteria before comparing models"). Ravi's: 19 of 20 WhatsApp orders with every item and quantity right, every reply under 10 seconds
- Then the same test for every brain. Our real test, internet cut, his 20 orders, every reply read by hand: the 1.1 GB brain got 8 of 20 (it kept writing "Sure, we'll have those ready for you within 30 minutes."), the 2.1 GB got 19 of 20 with every reply under 5 seconds, the 4.7 GB got 16 of 20 ("2 curd 2 milk" became "2 liters of curd and 2 liters of milk") and one reply took 11 seconds
- Keep the smallest one that passes: it's faster and needs less memory. Here that's the middle one, not the biggest
- A different job, a different winner: on Ravi's bills (EP38) the biggest is best, 18 of 20. Still not 20 of 20, so bills still get checked
- When the job changes, test again. Customers type Telugu words ("ara kilo panchadara and okati tea podi"), and on those 20 orders none of the three passed: 5, 14 and 12 of 20. The middle one confirmed a kilo for half a kilo; the biggest turned "2 perugu 2 paalu" into "2 eggs and 2 bananas"
- None passes? Change the prompt (for example, list the Telugu names of your items; we haven't tested that yet) or try another brain, then run the same test again

What this means for you:
1. Before you pick an AI, write down what "good enough" means for your job, with numbers
2. Give every AI you're considering the same 10-20 real tasks, with the right answers written down first
3. Keep the smallest, cheapest one that passes. Bigger isn't better at everything
4. New job, new inputs, new language: test again

How we tested (run by us): Qwen's own 4-bit GGUF files (Q4_K_M) of Qwen2.5-1.5B, 3B and 7B-Instruct, llama.cpp on a 4-core computer (no graphics card), greedy, the internet cut for every run. The pass mark was written into the test script before any brain ran. The prompt: "You are the WhatsApp assistant of a small grocery shop... confirm each item and say it will be delivered within 30 minutes. No prices. Reply in 2 short lines." 20 orders as typed, then the same 20 with Telugu words in English letters. Every reply read by hand; time = the whole reply. One machine, one prompt, 20 orders: a different prompt or a bigger online model can score differently. That's the point: your numbers belong to your job.
EP39's 17 seconds was the long reply; with "Reply in 2 short lines." the 4.7 GB brain writes in 6-11 seconds on this machine.
Sources: OpenAI, "Model selection" (developers.openai.com); Anthropic, "Choosing the right model" (platform.claude.com/docs).
