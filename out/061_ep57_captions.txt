Episode 57 of AI From Scratch: deploy your AI app (Season 6, a screen tutorial)
Deploying = moving the bot from your laptop to a computer that's always on. Three ways, priced on the companies' own pages (Nov 20, 2026; ₹95.25 a dollar, RBI reference rate of Nov 6):
1. The model on a GPU server, all month (Hugging Face, Nvidia T4 small): $288 = about ₹27,400 a month.
2. A free 2-core box (Hugging Face CPU Basic: 2 vCPU, 16 GB, free): ₹0. Our 7B model (8 GB) fits, and it answered in about 2 seconds, because shop answers are 3-4 words.
3. A tiny server (22 MB) that sends each message to an AI company's model: about ₹28 a month at 50 chats a day (OpenAI gpt-5.4-mini price: 235 tokens in, 4 out = 1.85 paise a chat; one model's price, others differ). EP50's 3.1 paise was a longer reply.
A GPU only pays off above about 50,000 chats a day.

The steps: 1) put the bot's code on a host, e.g. a free Hugging Face Space (CPU basic). 2) Any key goes in the Space's Settings > Variables and secrets > New secret, never in the code. 3) Give WhatsApp the Space's link: WhatsApp > Configuration > Callback URL (Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform docs).
The catch: a free Space sleeps after 48 hours with no visitors, and the next reply takes half a minute or more (our cold start: 24 s).

Try it before you pay for any server: tokens per chat × chats a day × your model's price. Ours: 1.85 paise a chat, ₹28 a month at 50 chats a day.

How we tested (Nov 20, 2026; pipeline/ep57/deploy_demo.py, committed before it ran): EP51's shop bot (the price list as the system prompt), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 4-bit (llama.cpp) on our computer. Self-hosted: 8,017 MB of memory, 24 s from a cold start to the first answer, 1.2-1.5 s a reply on 4 CPU cores and 1.7-2.3 s on 2 (all 5 answers right: ₹165, ₹340, out of stock, ₹84, ₹246). A tiny server that forwards to an API: 21.7 MB, starts in 0.2 s. We did not deploy to a real cloud from here, call a paid API or test a GPU. The tiny server (Python 3, no packages; key and prompt from the host's secrets):
import json, os, urllib.request
from http.server import BaseHTTPRequestHandler, HTTPServer
API, KEY, SYSTEM = os.environ["API_URL"], os.environ["API_KEY"], os.environ["SYSTEM"]
class H(BaseHTTPRequestHandler):
    def do_POST(self):
        msg = json.loads(self.rfile.read(int(self.headers["Content-Length"])))["text"]
        body = json.dumps({"model": os.environ.get("MODEL", "gpt-5.4-mini"), "messages": [{"role": "system", "content": SYSTEM}, {"role": "user", "content": msg}], "max_tokens": 60}).encode()
        req = urllib.request.Request(API, data=body, headers={"Content-Type": "application/json", "Authorization": "Bearer " + KEY})
        reply = json.loads(urllib.request.urlopen(req).read())["choices"][0]["message"]["content"]
        self.send_response(200); self.send_header("Content-Type", "application/json"); self.end_headers(); self.wfile.write(json.dumps({"reply": reply}).encode())
HTTPServer(("0.0.0.0", int(os.environ.get("PORT", 7860))), H).serve_forever()
(WhatsApp's webhook also needs its verify step and a reply call: Meta's "Create a webhook endpoint" docs.)
