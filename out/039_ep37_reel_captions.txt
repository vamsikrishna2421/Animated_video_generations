Episode 37 of AI From Scratch: quantization, how big AI models fit on small devices
- An AI model is billions of numbers. Full size, each one is stored with 16 bits: 65,536 possible values (exact to the paisa, in Ravi's terms)
- Quantization rounds every number to fewer bits: 8 bits = 256 values, 4 bits = 16 (the nearest ₹10), 2 bits = just 4 (₹0, ₹50, ₹100 or ₹150 on each shelf: biscuits become free)
- Our real test, the same small AI (Qwen2.5 1.5B) sorting 40 of the shop's WhatsApp messages: 16-bit (3.6 GB) 1 wrong · 8-bit (1.9 GB) 1 wrong · 4-bit (1.1 GB) 1 wrong · 2-bit (0.75 GB) 20 wrong (it filed nearly everything as an ORDER, "lunch is ready" included)
- 4-bit: a third of the size, the same answers, and a WhatsApp reply in 3.4 s instead of 7.5 s (the computer reads every number for every word, so fewer bytes means faster)

What this means for you:
1. Downloading a model (Ollama, LM Studio, Hugging Face)? Pick the file with Q4 in its name. Q4_K_M is the usual default: Ollama's qwen2.5:7b download is Q4_K_M, 4.7 GB (the same brain is 15.2 GB at 16-bit, too big for EP36's 8 GB card)
2. Room to spare? Q8 is fine, but in our test it gave the same answers as Q4 at 1.7x the size and 1.6x slower
3. Using the ChatGPT app? That runs on the company's computers; how they store their model is their choice

The catch: on the hardest job, bills, 4-bit lost a little (16-bit got 13 of 20 bills right, 4-bit 11). And bigger brains take squeezing better: our 7B at 2 bits still sorted 39 of 40.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
How we tested (run by us): Qwen's own GGUF files of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Apache 2.0) at 16, 8, 4, 3 and 2 bits, llama.cpp on a 4-core computer (server CPU, no graphics card), greedy decoding. 40 WhatsApp messages with the right label written down first (ORDER / PAYMENT / COMPLAINT / OTHER): 39, 39, 39, 38, 20 right. 20 bills against a 15-line price board: 13, 11, 11, 6, 3 right. The same WhatsApp reply: 7.5 s, 5.3 s, 3.4 s at 16, 8, 4 bits. The big brain (Qwen2.5-7B) at 2 bits, 3.0 GB: 39 of 40 sorted.
Why is 4-bit a third of the size, not a quarter? The "K" files keep a few parts at more bits (the sizes are Qwen's own files).
Don't phones run 2-bit AI? Yes, when the model is trained for it: Apple's ~3B on-device model uses "2-bit quantization-aware training" (Apple, 2025), and Google's Gemma 3 27B went from 54 GB to 14.1 GB with quantization-aware training (Google, Apr 2025). Squeezed after training, as in our test, 2 bits broke the small model.
