=== TITLE ===
Why Is AI Slow? 17-Second Reply vs 1 Second: Tokens, Latency, Cost (Tested) | AI From Scratch Ep 39 #Shorts

=== DESCRIPTION ===
Ravi anna's shop AI takes 17 seconds to answer a WhatsApp order, and the customer cancels. Two clocks: first the AI reads the whole message (the wait before the first word: latency), then it writes one token at a time (a token is a word or a piece of one: "sun·flower" is 2 tokens, "302" is 3). For every token, our laptop brain lifts its whole brain from memory: 4 tokens a second.

Our real test, internet cut, on a 4-core computer with no graphics card (Qwen2.5 7B, 4-bit): 4.3 s to read the order, 17.6 s for the 58-token reply. With "Reply in 2 short lines.": 35 tokens, 11.7 s. With a month of old chat in front (1,788 tokens): 92 s before the first word.

Online AIs on big graphics cards are far faster (Gemini 2.5 Flash: about 200 tokens a second, Artificial Analysis), but a bot pays for every token, and the reply's tokens usually cost 4-8x the message's (OpenAI and Google price lists): about 1-2 paise a reply.

What it means for you: ask for short replies; don't make the AI read what it doesn't need (a 2-line note about a customer, not the whole chat; a new chat for a new topic). Thinking models also bill their hidden thinking. Telugu takes more tokens than English for the same message (69 vs 37 on GPT-4o's tokenizer).

How we tested (run by us): Qwen's own GGUF files, llama.cpp, greedy, streamed token by token, median of 3 runs, every run offline.
Sources: OpenAI API pricing; Google Gemini API pricing; Artificial Analysis (Gemini 2.5 Flash); OpenAI "Hello GPT-4o" (tokenizer).
