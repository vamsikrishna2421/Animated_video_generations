=== TITLE ===
This AI Is 99.99% Sure This Dog Is a Banana: How AI Really Sees a Photo | AI From Scratch Ep 29

=== DESCRIPTION ===
A free photo AI looked at a golden retriever and said "golden retriever, 99.8%". We changed the photo's colours by a shade you can't see, and now it says "banana, 99.99% sure". Here is how AI actually sees a photo (computer vision), why that trick worked, and whether ChatGPT would fall for it. We ran every test ourselves.

In this episode:
- Pixels: a photo is a grid of pixels, and each pixel is 3 numbers (red, green, blue, from 0 to 255). Our small photo is 150,528 numbers; a 12 MP phone photo is about 36 million. Not one of them says "dog"
- Patterns: tiny pattern-finders (called filters) slide over the numbers, each looking for one thing, like an edge. You see two real ones from the AI, and what they light up on our photo
- Layers: edges build parts (eyes, ears), parts build a face, and out comes an answer: "golden retriever"
- Your turn: nudge just as many numbers, just as tiny, but randomly. Dog or banana? (Still dog, 5 tries out of 5)
- Why ours worked: the AI is free to download, so we looked inside it and moved 122,204 of its 150,528 input numbers by exactly 1, each toward "banana". Pushes that all aim the same way add up; random ones cancel out
- Would ChatGPT fall for it? Probably not: we aimed at this one AI, and ChatGPT is closed. Four other free photo AIs still said dog, and saving the photo as a JPEG broke the trick. But 99.99% sure was still wrong, and chatbots don't even show a number, so double-check what matters

How we tested (all free models, run by us): the photo is "Golden Retriever 88.JPG" by Mtlchung, CC0 (Wikimedia Commons), cropped to the 224 × 224 pixels the AI takes. The AI is ResNet-50 (microsoft/resnet-50, Apache-2.0, trained on ImageNet's 1.28 million labelled photos). The banana file comes from a targeted gradient attack (projected gradient descent, 80 steps) that may move each pixel value by at most 1 out of 255. Transfer check on the banana file: google/vit-base-patch16-224, microsoft/resnet-18, facebook/convnext-tiny-224 and microsoft/swin-tiny-patch4-window7-224 all say golden retriever. JPEG check: saved at quality 75 to 95, ResNet-50 says golden retriever again. The light-up maps are real first-layer responses, brightened so you can see them.
Background: Goodfellow, Shlens & Szegedy (2014), "Explaining and Harnessing Adversarial Examples".
