=== TITLE ===
Why AI Needs a Graphics Card (GPU): 1 Core vs 4 Cores, Tested | AI From Scratch Ep 36 #Shorts

=== DESCRIPTION ===
Diwali evening at Ravi anna's shop: 23 WhatsApp orders waiting, and the shop AI is still typing the first reply. Every word an AI writes is over a billion small multiplications. A CPU has a few fast cores; a graphics card (GPU) has thousands of small ones working at once: a gaming laptop's RTX 5060 has 3,328 cores and 8 GB of memory (NVIDIA's spec page).

Our real test, the same small AI writing the same reply on our laptop's CPU: 1 core 11.5 s, 2 cores 6.3 s, 4 cores 3.6 s. More cores make even one reply faster, because each word's work is split between them; 23 orders still take about 83 s. And the brain has to fit in the card's memory: the medium brain we tested is a 1.1 GB file and fits easily, but the big brain at full size is about 15 GB, an 8 GB card can't hold it, and the rest runs on the CPU again.

What it means for you: ChatGPT and the rest run on the companies' own graphics cards and AI chips, so you don't need one to use them. To run AI on your own machine, check the graphics card and its memory (GB), not the CPU's GHz. No card? Google Colab is free to use, with limited, not guaranteed GPU time.

How we tested (run by us): Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Qwen's 4-bit GGUF) with llama.cpp on a laptop CPU, the same reply every time, median of 3 runs. We tested no graphics card; the gaming-laptop scene is an illustration.
Sources: NVIDIA's RTX 50 series spec pages; Kaplan et al., "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models" (2020); Qwen2.5 model cards; Google Colab FAQ.
