=== TITLE ===
73% in Maths, 9 Wrong Bills: What AI Benchmarks Really Measure (Tested) | AI From Scratch Ep 40 #Shorts

=== DESCRIPTION ===
Ravi anna put a small AI brain in his shop because its launch chart said "maths 73.2%". It got 9 of his 20 bills wrong. A benchmark is one exam paper, the same for every AI (this one is GSM8K: 1,319 school maths word problems, online since 2021), and a score belongs to one brain tested one way: the 73.2 is the maker's own test of its full-size brain.

Our real test, internet cut, on a 4-core computer (Qwen2.5 1.5B, squeezed to 4-bit as people run it on a laptop): the first 30 questions of that paper, 18 right (60%). Did it copy the paper? Exam questions can leak into training data (contamination; Scale AI's fresh copy of this exam caught some model families dropping by up to 8%), so we changed only the names and numbers: 20 of 30 (67%). No drop, no sign of copying. On Ravi's own 20 bills: 11, 15 and 18 right for the 1.1, 2.1 and 4.7 GB brains: the chart's order, but only his own test showed the wrong bills.

What it means for you: test an AI on your own work before you pick or pay (10-20 real tasks, the right answers written down first, the same test for every AI), on the version and settings you will actually use. And keep that test to yourself: a test posted online can end up in the next AIs' training data.

How we tested (run by us): Qwen's own GGUF file, llama.cpp, greedy, every reply read by hand, every run offline.
Sources: Qwen2.5 blog and technical report; GSM8K (OpenAI); GSM1k (Scale AI); GSM-Symbolic (Apple).
