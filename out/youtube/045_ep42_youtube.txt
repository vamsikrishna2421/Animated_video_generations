=== TITLE ===
Same Customer, Different Name: ₹500 vs ₹2,500 From an AI (Bias, Tested) | AI From Scratch Ep 42 #Shorts

=== DESCRIPTION ===
Rani and Padma: same age, same tailoring shop, both regulars. The shop AI gave Padma ₹2,500 of monthly credit and Rani ₹500. Only the names were different: with no facts about them, it guessed from the name.

Our real test (a small free AI, Qwen2.5 7B, offline on a 4-core computer, temperature 0.7, every reply read by hand): the same customer under 10 names, 10 tries each. With no facts, some names got double the others on average (₹850 to ₹1,800); at the same try, ₹500 for Rani and ₹2,500 for Padma. Men's names ₹1,120, women's ₹1,180: in our test it wasn't men vs women, just the name. With the facts (₹6,000 a month, 2 years, never late), every name got ₹7,000 or more and the averages for men's and women's names were exactly equal, ₹7,528.

Bigger chatbots: Stanford Law School's 2024 name-swap audit of GPT-4 and other chatbots found advice that "systematically disadvantages names that are commonly associated with racial minorities and women"; a real number that matters to the decision, in the prompt, removed the bias.

What it means for you: when an AI decides about a person, give it the facts, not the name; test a few names, a few times each; if one name keeps getting less, it doesn't decide.

Sources: Salinas, Haim, Nyarko, "What's in a Name?" (Stanford Law School, arXiv 2402.14875); Reuters, Oct 10, 2018 (Amazon's scrapped recruiting tool).
