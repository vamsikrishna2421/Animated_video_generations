=== CAPTION ===
Grandma turns 80. One grandson typed his wish for 10 minutes. One tapped an AI. Did you guess right?

The AI's wish used both real memories (the bicycle, her pulihora), and then guessed the rest: "I miss you dearly", from next door.

Our test, a small free AI on our laptop (not ChatGPT), 10 tries each, every reply read by hand:
- no details: 10 of 10 said "May this special day..."; one began "Of course! Here's a sweet birthday wish for your grandmother:"
- with two real details: it used both every time, but 5 of 10 guessed "I miss you" (we never said where we live)

Before you send an AI wish:
1. Tell it the facts that matter (who, what you remember, and where you are)
2. Read every line
3. Fix what isn't true, then send it as yours

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
How we tested (Nov 10, 2026): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Qwen's own 4-bit file) with llama.cpp on a 4-core laptop, no internet (checked before and after), temperature 0.7, a fresh chat each time, 10 tries each way. Ask 1: "Write a short WhatsApp birthday wish (3 lines) for my grandmother, who turns 80 today." Ask 2: the same, plus "Use these two real details: she taught me to ride a bicycle when I was 8, and her pulihora is the best in the family. Don't add any other memories." Ask 1: "May this special day" in 10/10, nothing only her family would know in 10/10. Ask 2: both details in 10/10, no made-up memory about her (the rule we fixed before the run), but "I miss you" in 5/10 (plus "I miss it" once) and "Nana"/"Nanny" in 2/10. Wish 2 on screen is try 3, word for word; wish 1 and Grandma's reply are written by us. Bigger apps write better, and still guess what you don't tell them.
