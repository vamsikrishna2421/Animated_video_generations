########## ENGLISH (@ai_maastaaru) ##########
=== CAPTION ===
The same question: 13 tokens in English, 101 in Telugu (an older ChatGPT model's tokenizer). Today's ChatGPT: 23.

An AI doesn't read words; it reads tokens, chunks of text. Many tokenizers were built mostly from English text, so they cut Telugu letters into tiny pieces: the word "current" is 1 token in English and 12 written in Telugu (older tokenizer; 3 on today's).
Why it matters to you: an AI holds only so many tokens of a chat at once, so a long Telugu chat loses its start sooner. And apps that pay per token (OpenAI's API, for one, prices per million tokens) pay more for the same Telugu question.

Our test, five everyday questions (the bill, a birthday wish, a bank account, a school letter, a train refund), the same meaning in English and Telugu:
- older ChatGPT models (GPT-4, GPT-3.5): Telugu took 7.35x the tokens
- today's ChatGPT models (GPT-4o to GPT-5 share one tokenizer, per OpenAI's tiktoken library): 1.68x
- the free, open Qwen models (Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 count the same): 6.38x
- bonus: Telugu typed in English letters ("Ee nela naa current bill..."): about 1.5-1.8x on all of them
Getting fairer. Not fair yet.

What you can do: a long Telugu chat forgetting what you said? Ask it for a short summary and paste that into a new chat. Building with AI? Count your Telugu tokens on the model you'll use (each model cuts Telugu differently).
