########## ENGLISH (@ai_maastaaru) ##########
=== CAPTION ===
The same question: 13 tokens in English, 101 in Telugu (an older ChatGPT model's tokenizer). The newer one: 23.

An AI doesn't read words; it reads tokens, chunks of text. Many tokenizers were built mostly from English text, so they cut Telugu letters into tiny pieces: the word "current" is 1 token in English and 12 written in Telugu (older tokenizer).
Why it matters to you: in your app, the AI's memory is counted in tokens, so a long Telugu chat runs out of room sooner. Developers pay per token (OpenAI's API, for one, prices per million tokens), so the same Telugu question costs them more.

Our test, five everyday questions (the bill, a birthday wish, a bank account, a school letter, a train refund), the same meaning in English and Telugu:
- older ChatGPT tokenizer: Telugu took 7.35x the tokens
- newer ChatGPT tokenizer (2024): 1.68x
- a popular free open model (Qwen2.5): 6.38x
- bonus: Telugu typed in English letters ("Ee nela naa current bill..."): about 1.5-1.8x on all three
Getting fairer. Not fair yet.

What you can do: for long Telugu work, use a newer app. Paying per token? Count your Telugu tokens first.

How we counted (Nov 9, 2026): five questions written in English and in Telugu with the same meaning, counted with three real, open tokenizers: OpenAI's cl100k_base (used by its GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 models) and o200k_base (used by its GPT-4o-era models), via the tiktoken library, and the Qwen2.5 tokenizer (Hugging Face). Totals: English 66 tokens; Telugu 485 / 111 / 421; Telugu in English letters 117 / 101 / 117. Plain counting, no AI answers. Research: Petrov et al., "Language Model Tokenizers Introduce Unfairness Between Languages" (NeurIPS 2023): the same text can differ up to 15x in tokenized length across languages. Every count is in our repo.
