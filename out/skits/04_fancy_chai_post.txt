########## ENGLISH (@ai_maastaaru) ##########
=== CAPTION ===
Babu asked AI for a "fancy" post for his chai stall. His customers thought tea was ₹200 now.

The fix isn't "keep it simple". Don't tell the AI how to write: show it 3 of your own old posts and say "write like these".

The catch: it copies everything in them, old prices too. So say today's facts in the same message.

Works for office emails and WhatsApp messages too.

Our test (a small free AI model on a laptop, 10 tries each):
"Make it fancy" → 8 of 10 came out 5-star
"Keep it simple" → 0 of 10 used Babu's words
His 3 old posts + "same style, same Telugu-English mix" → 9 of 10 used his words, but 5 of 10 wrote last year's ₹10
Same + today's price → 10 of 10 said ₹15

=== COMMENT PROMPT ===
Before the reveal, what did you guess: A (₹15) or B (₹10)? Comment A or B.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
The prompt to copy:
"Here are 3 posts I wrote before:
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...
Write today's post in the same style and the same language mix as these. Today's special: ____, price ____."
Why it works: Anthropic's prompting guide says "Examples are one of the most reliable ways to steer Claude's output format, tone, and structure", and asks for varied examples so it doesn't "pick up unintended patterns" (like an old price). Google's Gemini guide: "We recommend to always include few-shot examples in your prompts", and warns that with too many examples "the model may start to overfit the response to the examples."
Our test: a free open model (Qwen2.5 7B, 4-bit, on a laptop CPU), 10 tries per prompt. Bigger chatbots write better Telugu-English, but the copying habit is the same, so check prices, dates and offers in anything it writes.

=== ALT_TEXT ===
Crayon-animated street skit at a roadside chai stall in Hyderabad. Raju, an auto driver, reads the stall's new post, "Indulge in the Spice of Life!", and asks if tea costs two hundred rupees now. Babu, the owner, had told the AI to make it fancy. "Keep it simple" gives "Hey there, tea lovers!", which sounds nothing like him. His daughter Swathi has him paste three of his own old posts and ask for the same style and Telugu-English mix. A one-question card asks what price the AI will write: his old posts say Rs 10, today's tea is Rs 15. The reply sounds like Babu but says Rs 10, last year's price. Swathi: it copies everything in your old posts, so put today's price in. The new post says Rs 15, with the rule on screen: show 3 old posts and say today's facts. Babu then types "Now make it fancy!", looping back to the start.
