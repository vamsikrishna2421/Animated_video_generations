=== CAPTION ===
Her power bill is usually ₹960. This month: ₹1,60,936. The app read one 6 as an 8.

Many power companies now let you send a photo of your meter, and some apps read it with AI. On a meter's display, a 6 is an 8 with one line missing: a little glare on that line, and the reading jumps.
Our test: we drew 24 meter photos (12 readings, clean and phone-like) and gave them to a small free AI that reads photos, offline (not the AI any power app uses). Meter digits, clean: 0 of 12 right (e.g. 66114 as 86111); phone-like: 0 of 12. The same 12 readings printed plainly: 12 of 12. It reads print; meter digits fooled it.

When the app shows a reading: check it against your meter, digit by digit, before you tap OK.
Already billed wrong? Complain to your power company with a dated photo of the meter.

(Kamala amma is made up; the misread "86111" is a real answer from our test.)

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
How we tested: we drew 24 meter photos ourselves so every true reading was known (a 5-digit seven-segment display; 12 readings, each drawn clean and again with a slight tilt, blur and a glare patch). A small free AI that reads photos, Qwen2.5-VL-3B (llama.cpp's own 4-bit file), on a 4-core computer, offline, temperature 0, was asked: "Read the kWh reading on this electricity meter's display. Answer with only the digits." Right: 0 of 12 clean, 0 of 12 phone-like (3 were off by one digit; 5 answers were empty). Then the same 12 readings in a plain printed font on the same meter: 12 of 12. Real meter-reading apps use other models; the habit (check the digits before you tap OK) carries over. Every photo and answer is in our repo.
