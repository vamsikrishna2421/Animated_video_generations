=== CAPTION ===
Chinnu was sitting right next to Amma when "he" called asking for ₹50,000.

Scammers can copy a voice from short clips posted online, then call with a fake emergency. Two things beat it:
1. A family code word, picked tonight, offline (not in the family group!)
2. If a call sounds like family but feels wrong: disconnect, then call back on the number you have saved.

Already sent money? Call 1930 (cyber crime helpline) or report at cybercrime.gov.in, fast.
Send this to your family group.

=== ALT_TEXT ===
Crayon-animated home skit: Amma gets a panicked call in her son Chinnu's voice asking for 50,000 rupees while Chinnu sits beside her eating dosa. She mutes the call; he explains AI copied his voice from his reels; she unmutes and asks the caller for their code word; the cloned voice stalls and she hangs up. Chinnu says to pick a real code word offline, and to disconnect and call back on his saved number. The helpline 1930 is on screen. The next day an unknown number calls and Amma answers, "Code word?"

=== SOURCES (for comments) ===
FBI IC3 PSA I-120324-PSA (Dec 3, 2024), "Criminals Use Generative Artificial Intelligence to Facilitate Financial Fraud": short audio clips of a loved one's voice; "Create a secret word or phrase with your family to verify their identity"; hang up and call back directly. https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203
FTC consumer alert (Mar 20, 2023), "Scammers use AI to enhance their family emergency schemes": "a short audio clip of your family member's voice — which he could get from content posted online"; "Call the person who supposedly contacted you and verify the story. Use a phone number you know is theirs."
India: National Cyber Crime Reporting helpline 1930 (MHA / Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre, since April 2022) and cybercrime.gov.in.
