=== CAPTION ===
Arjun was one click away from pasting 200 client phone numbers into an AI chatbot "to make the list neat".

Two myths most of us believe:
✗ "Only me and the AI see my chats." On personal accounts, your chats can be used to improve the AI, and some are read by human reviewers.
✗ "I'll just delete it." Google's own Gemini privacy page: chats a person reviewed are kept for up to 3 years, even after you delete your activity.

What to do instead:
1. Paste 3 fake rows, ask for the formula or the steps, run it on the real list yourself. The real data never leaves your laptop.
2. At work, use your company's approved AI account. Business plans don't train on your data by default (OpenAI's business-data page; Google Workspace says the same).
3. On a personal account, switch it off: ChatGPT: Settings > Data Controls > "Improve the model for everyone". Gemini: turn off "Keep Activity". Or use a temporary chat.

The rule: paste only what you'd put up on the office notice board.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
The 30-second check before you paste: names? phone numbers? salaries? passwords or OTPs? Aadhaar or PAN? client details? If yes, swap them for fake ones or don't paste.
The formula from the video, for a messy list: names =PROPER(TRIM(A2)), numbers =SUBSTITUTE(B2," ",""), then sort A to Z.
Facts: Google, Gemini Apps Privacy Hub (support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961, updated Oct 19, 2026): "Please don't enter confidential information that you wouldn't want a reviewer to see"; "A subset of chats are reviewed by human reviewers"; reviewed chats "are not deleted when you delete your activity" and are kept up to three years; turning off "Keep Activity" stops future chats being reviewed (those chats are kept 72 hours). OpenAI help center: saved chats follow the "Improve the model for everyone" setting; temporary chats aren't used to improve the models (a copy may be kept up to 30 days for safety).
