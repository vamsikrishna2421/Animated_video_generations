=== CAPTION ===
We asked an AI to plan our parents' 25th anniversary. It planned a hall for 200 guests. Nanna won't even take an auto.

An AI that knows nothing about your family fills the gaps with a guess. You often don't know which details it needs, so let it ask. Add one line to any "plan ..." request:
"Before you plan, ask me 3 questions."

Answer its questions, then it plans. One thing it will probably skip: your budget. Say it yourself.

Our real test, a small free AI (Qwen2.5 7B, offline, not ChatGPT), the same anniversary request, 10 tries each, every reply read by hand:
Just the request: 0 of 10 asked anything first. All 10 gave a generic party plan: a guest list or invitations, and a venue ("home, a restaurant or a hall"); 5 told us to hire a caterer, a chef, a band or a DJ.
With the line: 10 of 10 asked 3 questions before planning, mostly what our parents enjoy (9), the kind of celebration (8) and a special place (7). Only 1 asked about the budget.
After our answers (old songs for Amma, pesarattu for Nanna, our terrace, small, Rs 5,000): all 10 planned it at home with the songs and the pesarattu, 6 kept to the Rs 5,000 out loud, and 5 added a photo album. Not perfect: 4 still suggested paying a DJ, a band or a singer, and 1 switched to Chinese halfway.

The 3-lakh price in the cartoon is ours: the real AI never priced the party.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
"Why not just type all the details?" Do, when you know them. The line is for when you don't know what matters: it asks for what it needs. Our test AI asked about the budget only once in 10 tries, so add yours.
Big chatbots: in PCWorld's test (Mar 23, 2026) ChatGPT and Claude asked clarifying questions on their own, and Gemini did once told to. Tom's Guide suggests letting it ask up to three (Jun 4, 2026) and says the same kind of line worked in Claude, Gemini and most chatbots it tried (Jul 8, 2026).
