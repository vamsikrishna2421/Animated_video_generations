=== CAPTION ===
Two voice notes from "Kittu". One is an AI copy of his voice, made from 13 seconds of an old voice note. Could you tell?

Our test: Kittu is a computer voice (we did not copy a real person, on purpose). A voice-cloning service copied him from 3.6, 13 and 31 seconds of his voice note; each copy took a few seconds to make. A voice-matching program, comparing with another sentence Kittu said: real Kittu 62, the copy from 13 s 62, the copy from 3.6 s 50, a stranger 7. That's a program's score, not human ears.

It isn't rare: in McAfee's 2023 survey of 1,010 Indian adults, 47% had faced an AI voice scam or knew someone who had, and 48% of those who lost money lost over ₹50,000.

Try it today: agree a family code word with your parents, in person, and never post it. Any call or voice note asking for money: cut it, call the saved number, ask the code word. Report fraud on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
How we tested (Nov 18, 2026): "Kittu" and "a stranger" are computer voices (Parler-TTS, on our machine). Fish Audio's voice cloning (the service this channel uses for its narrator) copied Kittu from the first 3.6 s, 13.2 s and 30.6 s of his voice note. SpeechBrain's ECAPA speaker-recognition model scored each voice against two sentences Kittu said that no reference contained (cosine similarity ×100): Kittu vs Kittu 62; copies 50 (3.6 s), 62 (13 s), 65 (31 s); a stranger 7. Telugu (13.6 s): Kittu 54, copy 58, stranger 16. Copies took 2.7-9.2 s to make. A program's score is not a test of human listeners. Sources: McAfee "Beware the Artificial Impostor" (May 2023; reported by Business Today and Business Insider India); the family code word and call-back advice: US FTC's family-emergency-scam alert; India's cyber fraud helpline 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in.
