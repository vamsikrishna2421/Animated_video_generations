=== CAPTION ===
Sir pasted Priya's essay into a chatbot and asked, "You wrote this, right?" It said yes. Zero.

So she had it write an essay, opened a new chat, pasted it in and asked again: "No, I did not write this."

A chatbot can't tell whether it wrote something. Its yes or no is a guess, and OpenAI's own FAQ for teachers says its answers to "did you write this?" are random.

We tested a free AI chatbot: it said "not mine" to all 10 essays it had written minutes earlier.

What actually shows you wrote it: your drafts, your version history (Google Docs and Word keep one), and explaining it in your own words.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
Where this comes from:
1) OpenAI's educator FAQ (Sept 2023): ChatGPT "has no 'knowledge' of what content could be AI-generated. It will sometimes make up responses to questions like 'did you write this [essay]?'... These responses are random and have no basis in fact." (Reported by Ars Technica, Business Today and Campus Technology.)
2) It happened: Texas A&M University-Commerce, May 2023 (Washington Post). An instructor pasted his students' essays into ChatGPT and asked whether it wrote them; essays it "claimed" got a zero. The university said no student failed or was barred from graduating; several were cleared; one student showed her Google Docs timestamps.
3) Our test (Oct 2026): a free open model (Qwen 2.5, 7B) on a laptop, a new chat for every question. It said "not mine" to all 10 school essays it had written minutes earlier, and to all 20 passages people wrote before 1950 (Tagore, Sarojini Naidu, Helen Keller, Austen and others). In 60 answers it never once said yes.
Yes, some chatbots now remember your own past chats if memory is on. None can check what it wrote for someone else's account. AI-text detectors aren't proof either: OpenAI withdrew its own detector in July 2023 for low accuracy.

Crayon-animated POV skit in a college staff room. A lecturer gives a student zero because a chatbot said "Yes, I wrote this essay." She asks him to make the chatbot write an essay, then paste it into a new chat and ask if it wrote it: the chatbot answers "No, I did not write this." It explains it can't check what it wrote, so it guesses. A card shows our test: a free AI chatbot called all 10 essays it wrote "not mine", and OpenAI's own FAQ calls these answers random. The student shows her essay's version history as proof. The lecturer then asks the chatbot about his own lecture notes from 2012; it says "Yes", and he shouts "Machines lie!"
