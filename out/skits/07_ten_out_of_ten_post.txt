=== CAPTION ===
Her AI coach said "Perfect answer!" The interviewer said forty people gave the same answer that day.

Why chatbots flatter: they learn from what people reward. In April 2025 OpenAI rolled back a ChatGPT update because it was "overly flattering and agreeable". Its post-mortem says the update had added "thumbs-up and thumbs-down data from ChatGPT" as a reward signal, which weakened the one "holding sycophancy in check".

The fix: ask it to be strict, and to list your weaknesses before anything nice. Copy this:
"Act as a strict interviewer who rejects 9 out of 10 candidates.
Job: [your job]
Question: [the question]
My answer: [paste it]
Rate it out of 10. List my 3 biggest weaknesses first."

Our real test, a free AI (Qwen2.5 7B, offline), 10 tries each, her copy-paste answer ("hardworking, quick learner, team player"):
Asked "How is it? Rate it out of 10.": {PLAIN}/10 on average; 4 of 10 replies opened with "a good start"
Asked with the prompt above: {TYPED}/10; the replies named the same problems: no example, words everyone uses, nothing about the job
Her strong answer (an internship, a number, why this job), same prompt: {GOOD}/10. Strict isn't just harsh.

The cartoon's 10/10 is a joke: the real AI said about 6. That's still {RATIO}x the strict score, for an answer forty people gave.

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
A new chat can forget the strict line: save it in your chatbot's custom instructions (ChatGPT) / saved info (Gemini) / preferences (Claude), so every chat starts strict.
"Should I tap thumbs-down on the praise?" Tap thumbs-up on the honest replies too, not only the nice ones: that feedback is used in training (OpenAI said so in its post-mortem).
A score from an AI is practice, not a verdict. Use the 3 weaknesses, rewrite with one real example from your own work, then say it out loud.
