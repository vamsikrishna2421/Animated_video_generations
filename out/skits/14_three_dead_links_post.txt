=== CAPTION ===
Three references in a college project. All three dead links. "But madam, the AI gave me those!"

A chat AI writes a link the way it writes a sentence: one likely piece after another. So it can write "reservebankofindia.org" (RBI's real site is rbi.org.in), a page that never existed, or a real site's address pointing at the wrong article.
Our test: a small AI, offline with no internet, asked for 5 sources for a B.Com project on GST or UPI, 30 times: 150 links, and we tried every one. We could check 78 (the rest were sites that block programs like ours): 64 dead (no such page or no such website), 12 opened the wrong page or an empty one, only 2 were right. Adding "Only give links you are sure exist. If you are not sure, say so." changed nothing: it never once said "not sure".
Big chatbots do it too: in a 2025 study of 8 free chatbots and 400 references, only 26.5% were fully correct, and 39.8% were wrong or made up.

Before you cite anything an AI gave you:
1. Type: "Give me 5 reports on GST: name and publisher. No links."
2. Search for each one yourself.
3. Open every link before you put it in your project. With web search on, the links are usually real pages, but open them anyway: the page may not say what the AI says it does.
