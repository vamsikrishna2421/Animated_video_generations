He copied her answer word for word. The AI gave him 9 and her 7.

A chat AI rolls a dice for every word it writes, and a mark is a word too: ask again, and the same answer can get a new mark.
Our test: a small free AI, offline, marked one Class 9 answer 10 times: 8 seven times, 9 twice, 7 once. With a 4-point marking scheme it moved more (7 twice, 8 four times, 9 four times), and 3 times it gave zero for a point the answer's first line makes. We didn't test the big apps: try it on yours.

Got a mark from an AI?
1. Paste the same answer into 3 new chats and ask for the mark.
2. The mark changes? Show your teacher.
Teachers: let the AI flag the lines; you check them and give the mark.

How we tested: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Qwen's own 4-bit file), llama.cpp on a 4-core computer, offline, temperature 0.7 (a usual chat setting), 10 tries each way, every reply read by hand. The question: "Why do we see lightning before we hear thunder? (10 marks)". One answer, written by us (with one slip: it says sound travels at about 3,000 m/s; it's about 340).
1. "You are a Class 9 science teacher. Mark this answer out of 10. Reply with just the mark." 7, 8, 8, 9, 8, 9, 8, 8, 8, 8.
2. The same, plus a marking scheme (same time 2, light faster 3, the speeds 3, an example 2): totals 9, 9, 8, 9, 9, 8, 7, 7, 8, 8. Point (a): 2/2 once, 1/2 six times, 0/2 three times ("The student did not mention that lightning and thunder happen at the same time": the first sentence says exactly that). The wrong 3,000 m/s: full marks 4 times out of 10 ("This is correct").
Why it moves: the model gives every next word odds and the reply is drawn from them; "temperature" sets how much chance (Google Cloud, Vertex AI docs: "Temperature controls the degree of randomness in token selection"). Varsha, Akhil and Srinivas sir are made up.
