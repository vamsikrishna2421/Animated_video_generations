=== CAPTION ===
A new AI "designs a part in 4 seconds". Kiran believed it. Meghana asked one question: says who?

SKY-001 from Skyfall AI (launched Oct 19) is pitched as "the first engineering world model". Per the company, it imagines the shape first, splits it into parts down to the screws, then draws each part the way a CAD designer does: sketch, stretch, cut.

Their own test against GPT-6 Astra, on 3D CAD parts:
Time per design: 3.9 s vs 199 s (51x faster)
Cost per design: $0.005 vs $0.75 (150x cheaper)
Shape match: 79.7% vs 79.1% (a tie)

On floor plans: 1.22 s vs 144.4 s, $0.00237 vs $0.4465, and a shape match of 43.6% vs 40.6%. No model in their test reached 50%.

So the real claim is speed and cost. That matters to teams making thousands of parts or plans; for one part, 3 minutes and 75 cents is fine. It's the usual pattern for specialist models: they beat the giants on one narrow job, mostly on speed and cost. Their $0.005 is cost per design in their test, not a price you can pay: it's waitlist only, with no public price and no outside test yet.

Before you believe any AI launch, ask:
1. Who tested it? (SKY-001: they did)
2. Against what, on which task? (their own tasks)
3. Can I use it today? (not yet)

=== PINNED_COMMENT ===
Every number is from Skyfall AI's own launch page (skyfall.ai, Oct 19, 2026); no outside group has tested SKY-001 yet. The launch post on X says "51-125x faster, 150-210x cheaper"; the two tables on the site show 51x and 118x faster, and 150x and 188x cheaper, than GPT-6 Astra (the site's summary boxes also round the floor-plan figures to 119x and 186x). The 125x and 210x aren't in those tables. "Shape match" is IoU (intersection over union): the overlap between the AI's shape and the right shape, divided by both together. Next best in their CAD table: 69.4%. On floor plans a different model came second (42.3%), ahead of GPT-6 Astra.
