=== CAPTION ===
We gave an AI helper our phone's manual and asked: "Will my phone survive a shower?" Its keyword search pulled up the camera's meteor shower mode. It matched the word, not the meaning. On 30 everyday questions, keyword search put the right page first 18 times; meaning search 25.

Episode 32 of AI From Scratch: how AI finds the right page in your documents (RAG, part 2)
- Why it matters: with no page, AI guesses. Asked "Can I give it back if I just don't like it?", our small free AI said yes, for a refund. The manual says no.
- A short document? Just paste it. Real manuals run to hundreds of pages; in our test, whole chapters found the right page as often as headings (25/30) but sent 6x the text with every question, and AI services charge by the word
- Step 1, cut it into pieces: every 40 words, even mid-sentence: 13/30. The same pieces overlapping: 20. Cut at headings: 25
- Step 2, a vector database: each piece stored as numbers for its meaning, a library sorted by meaning; your question finds its shelf
- Step 3, a re-ranker reads your question beside each top piece and reorders them: first-place misses went from 5 to 3. It's slower (about 80 ms vs under 1 ms a question), so it pays off when the AI reads only one piece (in the top 3, meaning search alone already had the right page 30/30)

Your turn, answered: the re-ranker. If your AI reads only the first piece and the right page keeps coming second, a bigger AI still reads the wrong page. Ours moved "How long is the guarantee?" from the returns page to the warranty page.

Short document? Paste it. Big one? Cut at headings, search by meaning, re-rank if it reads one piece. Using a chatbot with your PDF? Ask it to quote the lines it used.

How we tested: a made-up 45-section manual for a made-up phone (2,239 words) and 30 questions written the way people ask. A hit = the piece the search puts first contains the answer sentence. Keyword search = BM25; meaning search = bge-small-en-v1.5 (BAAI, MIT); re-ranker = ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Apache-2.0), all free, on a 4-core CPU, no GPU. Answers by SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct (Hugging Face). With section pieces: keyword 18/30, meaning 25/30, re-ranked 27/30 (in the top 3: 26, 30, 29). 30 questions on one manual is a small test. === SOURCES (for comments) ===
Demo (run by us; pipeline/ep32 in the project): made-up K7 phone manual, 45 sections, 8 chapters, 2,239 words; 30 questions; hit = the first piece contains the answer sentence.
Right page first, out of 30 (keyword / meaning / meaning + re-ranker): whole chapters 23 / 25 / 28 (about 6x the words); sections 18 / 25 / 27; single sentences 11 / 16 / 20; every 40 words 11 / 13 / 15; every 40 words with a 20-word overlap 12 / 20 / 22.
Models: BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 (MIT, 33.4M parameters), cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Apache-2.0, 22.7M), HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct (Apache-2.0). Re-ranking 10 pieces took about 74 ms a question on the CPU; meaning search under 1 ms.
Retrieve and re-rank: Sentence-Transformers documentation (sbert.net): a fast retriever narrows millions of documents to about 100 candidates; a cross-encoder re-ranker scores each (question, document) pair, more accurately but too slowly for the whole collection.
Vector database example: FAISS (Meta FAIR), "a library for efficient similarity search and clustering of dense vectors", for sets of vectors of any size.
Long inputs: Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts", arXiv 2307.03172 (2023, TACL): models used information at the start or end of a long input best and often missed it in the middle.
