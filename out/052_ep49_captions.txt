Episode 49 of AI From Scratch: AI and jobs, what really changes (the Season 5 finale)
- Our test: we gave a small free AI (Qwen2.5 7B, 4-bit, offline: weaker than the big free apps) the five paper tasks from a shop helper's day, five tries each, and read every reply. 19 of 25 right. Sums, GST and stock lists: 15 of 15. Replies to a customer 4 of 5. Offer messages ready to send: 0 of 5. All five were good drafts, but each had a blank like "[Your Shop Name]" or a stray word to fix. Our prompt never gave it the shop's name.
- Honest notes: in the first run our own 160-token limit cut off 4 of the 5 totals and 1 reorder list mid-working; one rule for both: those two tasks were re-run with room, 5 tries each (committed before they ran): 5 of 5 and 5 of 5.
- It can't carry sacks, deliver, or know the regulars. Most jobs are a bundle of tasks like this.
- Worldwide, one job in four has tasks generative AI could do (ILO, May 2025). The ILO expects most of those jobs to change rather than vanish, and clerical jobs to change the most. These figures are what could change, not job losses.

What changes for you:
1. List your tasks. Let AI draft and add the paper ones
2. Give it the context (the shop's name, the date), then check before you send
3. Keep the work that needs hands and people. If your job is all paper, be the one who checks the AI, and learn the tool
