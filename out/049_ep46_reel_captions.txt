Episode 46 of AI From Scratch: copyright and AI
- Who wins is the contest's rule. Who owns it is the law's
- USA: an AI picture won a digital-art prize at the 2022 Colorado State Fair, made with 624 prompts. The US Copyright Office refused to register it because the artist claimed the AI-made parts too (2023); he has sued
- US Copyright Office (Jan 2025): "prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make users of an AI system the authors of the output". Your own drawing, edits and arrangement can be protected
- India: the Copyright Act says the author of a computer-generated work is "the person who causes the work to be created" (Section 2(d)(vi)). No court has tested that on today's AI, and the government's paper on AI outputs isn't out yet
- India's DPIIT committee (Dec 2025) proposed that AI companies pay royalties for training on copyrighted work: a proposal, not law

Using AI in work you'll enter, sell or show:
1. Say you used it (contests, clients)
2. Add your own drawing, edits, layout: that part is yours
3. Keep your drafts and layered files as proof
